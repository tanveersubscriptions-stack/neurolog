Why these variables?

Sleep → Memory

The hippocampus consolidates memories during slow-wave sleep. Less sleep = fewer memories transferred to long-term storage.

Phone → Attention

Constant notifications fragment attentional networks in the prefrontal cortex, reducing sustained focus capacity.

Stress → Focus

Elevated cortisol impairs PFC function — the region governing working memory and executive attention.

tip

After several weeks of logging, use this section to frame your personal data within established neuroscience findings — exactly what the project prompt asks for.

01

Sleep & memory consolidation

Walker et al., 2017 — Why We Sleep

Adults sleeping fewer than 6 hours show up to a 40% reduction in memory consolidation. The hippocampus requires slow-wave sleep to replay and encode episodic memories.

Prediction for your data Sleep ≥ 7 hrs → memory recall score 7–10/10
02

Smartphone presence & cognitive capacity

Ward et al., 2017 — Journal of the Association for Consumer Research

The mere presence of a smartphone reduces available working memory. Daily use above 4 hours is associated with poorer sustained attention on standardized tasks.

Prediction for your data Phone > 4 hrs → focus drops below 6/10
03

Stress, cortisol & prefrontal function

Arnsten, 2015 — Nature Reviews Neuroscience

Acute stress elevates norepinephrine and dopamine in the PFC, impairing working memory. Chronic cortisol exposure has been shown to physically reduce PFC grey matter density.

Prediction for your data Stress > 7/10 → focus < 5/10 on same day
04

Writing your comparison

After collecting data, look for: correlation direction (does more sleep track with better memory?), outliers (days that break the pattern — why?), and confounding variables (caffeine, exercise, illness).

Noting confounds in your write-up shows genuine scientific thinking — the kind that stands out in applications.
